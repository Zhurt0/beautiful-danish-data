Life course in the registers

A person living in Denmark leaves a trace in the registers at almost every turn of life. The chart follows one life from birth to death: the numbered events along the top are the main things that happen to people, and each row below is a register. A grey bar shows the ages over which a register follows a person, and a dot marks an event that the register records.

Ages are typical ages, for illustration. What exists also depends on the calendar year: monthly payroll (BFL) starts in 2008, prescriptions (LMDB) in 1995, weekly transfers (DREAM) in 1991, hospital records (LPR) in 1977 with outpatients from 1995, and the crime registers in 1980. Paper counts refer to the 155 papers on this site. Income (IND), wealth (FORM) and labour-market status (AKM) are also updated every year whether or not anything happens.

Events and the registers that record them

#EventTypical agesRegisters and what they record
1Birth
A person enters the registers the day they are born in Denmark.		0
  • Births (MFR): Birth weight, gestational age and Apgar score, linked to the mother
  • Population (BEF): A personal number (PNR) is created, with mother's and father's PNR and municipality
  • Hospital (LPR): The birth stay and any neonatal admissions
  • Migration and addresses: First address
2Childhood health care
Hospital visits and medicines from the first years of life.		0–15
  • Hospital (LPR): Every hospital contact with diagnoses (ICD-10) and dates
  • Prescriptions (LMDB): Every prescription filled at a pharmacy (ATC code, date, amount)
3Starting school
School enrolment starts the education record.		5–7
  • Education (UDDA): Enrolment spells by programme (KOTRE) from the first school year
4Parents separate or the family moves
Changes in the household while growing up.		0–17
  • Population (BEF): Family ID and family type change; parents' marital status
  • Migration and addresses: Each move, with date, municipality and parish
5End of compulsory school
Lower secondary school is completed.		15–17
  • Education (UDDA): Primary and lower secondary becomes the highest completed education (HFAUDD)
6First job
Student jobs and the first regular job link the person to an employer.		15–25
  • Labour market (IDA): Employer (CVRNR), workplace, occupation (DISCO) and hourly wage for the November job
  • Monthly payroll (BFL): Monthly pay, paid hours and job start date (from 2008)
  • Labour status (AKM/RAS): Socio-economic status changes to employed
  • Income (IND): Wage income for the year
  • Employer's firm registers: The employer's industry, size, accounts and trade, through the firm-worker link
7Upper secondary or vocational school
Gymnasium or a vocational programme.		16–20
  • Education (UDDA): Programme spells, start and completion dates
8Contact with the police
Danish criminal responsibility starts at 15; charges peak in the late teens and twenties.		15–30
  • Crime (KRAF/KRSI): Charges and convictions with offence code, decision type and dates
9Leaving home
Moving out of the parents' household.		18–24
  • Population (BEF): A new family ID, no longer counted in the parents' family
  • Migration and addresses: New address and move date
10Higher education
University or professional degree.		19–28
  • Education (UDDA): Programme, start and completion dates, new highest education
  • Transfers (DREAM): Weeks on the state study grant (SU)
11Arriving in Denmark
For immigrants, the record starts on arrival rather than at birth.		0–60
  • Population (BEF): Origin type (immigrant, descendant) and country of origin
  • Migration and addresses: Immigration date and first address
  • Education (UDDA): Foreign qualifications are only partly covered; Statistics Denmark filled gaps with a survey (see the Survey page)
12Moving in together or marrying
Forming a couple.		24–35
  • Population (BEF): Partner's PNR, marital status and a shared family ID
  • Migration and addresses: Shared address
13Having children
The person now appears as a parent, and the child's records link back to them.		25–40
  • Births (MFR): Recorded as the mother or father; the child's birth outcomes
  • Population (BEF): The child is linked through MOR_PNR / FAR_PNR
  • Hospital (LPR): Pregnancy and birth admissions for the mother
  • Transfers (DREAM): Weeks of parental leave benefit
  • Monthly payroll (BFL): Monthly hours and pay around the birth
14Buying a home
Becoming a home owner.		25–40
  • Wealth (FORM): Property value, mortgage and bank debt at year end
  • Income (IND): Interest expenses
  • Migration and addresses: Move date and new address
15Changing job or being promoted
Moving between employers or up the hierarchy.		20–60
  • Labour market (IDA): New employer and workplace, occupation (DISCO), tenure
  • Monthly payroll (BFL): Exact start and end dates of each job
  • Employer's firm registers: The new employer's characteristics
  • Income (IND): Change in earnings
16Separation or divorce
A couple splits up.		30–55
  • Population (BEF): Marital status and family ID change; the partner link ends
  • Migration and addresses: One partner moves out
  • Wealth (FORM): Assets and debts now recorded separately
17Job loss and unemployment
Losing a job and searching for a new one.		20–64
  • Monthly payroll (BFL): Job end date; monthly pay stops
  • Transfers (DREAM): Weeks on unemployment insurance or social assistance
  • Labour market (IDA): Degree of unemployment over the year
  • Labour status (AKM/RAS): Status changes to unemployed
  • Income (IND): Public transfers received
18Illness and sick leave
A health shock during working life.		20–65
  • Hospital (LPR): Diagnoses and admission dates
  • Prescriptions (LMDB): Prescriptions filled
  • Transfers (DREAM): Weeks on sickness benefit
  • Monthly payroll (BFL): Hours and pay during and after the illness
19Disability pension or early retirement
Leaving the labour market before pension age.		50–64
  • Transfers (DREAM): Weeks on disability pension or voluntary early retirement
  • Labour status (AKM/RAS): Status changes to outside the labour force
  • Income (IND): Transfer income
20Retirement
Reaching pension age.		62–70
  • Transfers (DREAM): Public old-age pension
  • Income (IND): Pension income
  • Wealth (FORM): Pension wealth (later years)
  • Labour status (AKM/RAS): Status changes to retired
  • Monthly payroll (BFL): The last month of pay
21Health care in old age
Hospital use and medicines rise steeply.		70–90
  • Hospital (LPR): Hospital contacts and diagnoses
  • Prescriptions (LMDB): Prescriptions filled
22Death
The record ends.		60–95
  • Deaths (DOD): Date of death and underlying and contributing causes (ICD-10)
  • Population (BEF): The person leaves the population
  • Hospital (LPR): The last hospital contacts
