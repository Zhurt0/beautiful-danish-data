Variable map

Statistics Denmark delivers data as registers, one per topic, that all share the personal identifier (PNR) on the individual side and the firm identifier (CVRNR) on the firm side. The two sides are joined through the employer link in IDA, FIDA or the monthly payroll data. The map below lists the registers behind the papers on this site, the variables that matter most, and which papers use each register.

Variable names follow Statistics Denmark's documentation of high-quality variables, but names and version suffixes (such as _13) change between years and projects. Treat the codes as pointers and check the current documentation at dst.dk before writing code.

More on this register: history, content, variables and pitfalls

The National Patient Register (Landspatientregisteret, LPR) records every contact with a Danish hospital. It was set up in 1977 so that the health authority could monitor hospital use and plan capacity, and has had complete national coverage since 1978. It started with inpatients on somatic (non-psychiatric) wards and was extended step by step to outpatient, emergency and psychiatric contacts and to private hospitals. Today it is run by the Danish Health Data Authority (Sundhedsdatastyrelsen) and is also used to pay hospitals, monitor disease and quality, and for research. Lynge et al. 2011; Schmidt et al. 2015; Health Data Authority, register page

At a glance

Unit
One record per hospital contact: an admission or an outpatient contact. Linked to a person by the CPR number. Health Data Authority, extract guide 2022
Starts
1977 (somatic inpatients); complete national coverage from 1978. Lynge et al. 2011; Schmidt et al. 2015
Coverage
8,085,603 different people had at least one record in 1977–2012, out of 8,342,199 people who lived in Denmark at some point in those years. Greenland and the Faroe Islands are not covered. Schmidt et al. 2015
Diagnoses
ICD-8 (a Danish version) until the end of 1993, ICD-10 from 1994. ICD-9 was never used in Denmark. Schmidt et al. 2015
Not in it
General practice, medicine bought at pharmacies, test results, causes of death and vital status. These sit in other registers. Health Data Authority, extract guide 2022; Schmidt et al. 2015
Run by
The Danish Health Data Authority. Researchers reach the data through its Research Service (Forskerservice) or through Statistics Denmark. Health Data Authority, register page; Statistics Denmark, data from other sources

How it grew

The register did not start as the all-contacts register it is today. Each extension is also a break in any series that runs across it.

YearWhat changed

1987First revision of the admission data. Admission type (acute or planned) and the patient types 'outpatient' and 'emergency department patient' date from here. A detailed registration of accidents is added. Schmidt et al. 2015

What to watch out for

The Scandinavian Journal of Public Health article calls the register overall a sound data source, and warns that its content and the definitions of single variables have changed over time and that changes in how hospitals are organised affect what is registered and how completely. For an economist who uses hospital contacts as a shock or an outcome, these are the points that matter most. Lynge et al. 2011

  1. A diagnosis is only as good as its code. The share of registered diagnoses confirmed by the medical record (the positive predictive value) ranges from below 15% to 100% across 253 validated codes and algorithms. Heart attack is close to 100% in recent years; many others are much lower. Look up the validation study for the code you use. Schmidt et al. 2015
  2. Only what reaches a hospital is there. Heart attacks and hip fractures are captured almost completely. Conditions mostly treated by a GP, such as hypertension, uncomplicated diabetes or mild depression, are captured poorly, and the hospital cases are the severe ones. Schmidt et al. 2015; Mors et al. 2011
  3. 1994 and 1995 are breaks. The move to ICD-10 in 1994 and the arrival of outpatient and emergency contacts in 1995 create jumps in any long series. Recorded alcoholic cirrhosis was flat in 1988–1993, rose 32% in 1994 and another 10% once outpatient and emergency visits counted. Statistics Denmark recommends grouped diagnosis variables rather than detailed codes across 1994. Schmidt et al. 2015; Statistics Denmark, ADIAG
  4. Patient types were redefined. The codes for inpatient, outpatient and emergency patients changed in 1987, 2002 and 2014, so counts of admissions, outpatient visits or emergency visits are not comparable across those years without recoding. Statistics Denmark reports a break in outpatient and emergency counts between 2013 and 2014. Schmidt et al. 2015; Statistics Denmark, hospital use documentation
  5. 2019 is a break, and a weak year. LPR2 and LPR3 data cannot be compared directly. Admission counts are not comparable between 2018 and 2019, and 2019 is not comparable with later years or between regions. About 9% of reports were in error in autumn 2019; by March 2020 the share of contacts with errors had fallen to 1.5%. Statistics Denmark, hospital use documentation; Health Data Authority, LPR3 news 2019; Health Data Authority, letter October 2019; Health Data Authority, letter May 2020
  6. The first record is not the onset. Nothing exists before 1977, and outpatient diagnoses only from the mid-1990s. Treating the first recorded contact as a new case overstates incidence in early years; a wash-out period is advised. Schmidt et al. 2015
  7. Payment and diagnostic practice move the data. DRG payment from 2002 may have made registration more complete and changed coding. New diagnostic criteria do the same: a rise in heart attack admissions in 2000–2004 is attributed to the troponin test introduced in 2000. Schmidt et al. 2015
  8. Private hospitals are thin. Their reporting is considered incomplete even though it has been compulsory since 2003, least complete for privately paid services. They produced 2.2% of hospital activity in 2010. Schmidt et al. 2015
  9. Extracts change. The register is corrected continuously, so two extracts taken at different times can differ, and record numbers change with corrections. Health Data Authority, extract guide 2022
  10. Administrative fields are more reliable than diagnoses. In a 1990 national sample of 1,094 discharges the primary diagnosis was correct at full detail in 73% of cases and at three digits in 81%, while admission type was wrong in 1.4% and discharge date in 1.5%. A 2003 sample found dates, hospital codes and CPR numbers accurate. Schmidt et al. 2015

Access and linkage

  • Linkage. The CPR number links each contact to every other register: income, the labour market database, education, prescriptions and causes of death. Schmidt et al. 2015
  • At Statistics Denmark. Statistics Denmark holds LPR2 data for 1977–2019 and LPR3 data from 2019 that can be ordered for a project. The Health Data Authority releases its data to a Statistics Denmark project only after Statistics Denmark has approved the project. Statistics Denmark, data from other sources
  • Psychiatry is separate. Psychiatric contacts (from 1995) sit in their own tables and have to be requested as such. Health Data Authority, extract guide 2022; Statistics Denmark, variable lists
  • Ask for both code systems. A request for a diagnosis or procedure over the whole period has to list both the ICD-8 and the ICD-10 codes, and both procedure classifications. Health Data Authority, extract guide 2022
  • Hospital prices. DRG tariffs are not part of the register but can be linked to a contact through the record number; Statistics Denmark holds them for 2002–2018. Statistics Denmark, data from other sources

The access rules described in the published articles are those of 2011 and 2015. The current application procedure was not checked for this page.
