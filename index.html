Why this page

Denmark links every resident, firm, hospital visit, tax return and job to a single identifier, and lets researchers work with the whole population from the 1980s onwards. This page is a reading list of the economics that has come out of that infrastructure in the top general-interest journals and in the two leading field journals for labour and public economics: what question each paper asks, what it finds, and which registers it is built from.

The list covers 158 papers. It was assembled by searching Crossref for every article in these journals whose title or abstract mentions Denmark or Danish data, and by going through the publication lists of the main Danish research groups and authors, then checking each paper. It excludes AEA Papers and Proceedings, finance journals and papers that only use Danish data as one country among many. It will still miss papers whose abstracts do not name the country, so additions are welcome. The Themes page regroups the same papers by topic, and the Variable map lists the registers and variables they rely on, at the individual and the firm level. If a paper is missing or a summary is wrong, open an issue or a pull request on GitHub.

Subjective Earnings Risk

Andrew Caplin, Victoria Gregory, Eungik Lee, Søren Leth-Petersen and Johan Sæverud · Review of Economic Studies, 2026 · Individual

Introduces a survey instrument for earnings risk that allows for quitting and being fired, fielded to Danish workers and linked to their register earnings histories. Job transitions drive subjective risk, which is many times smaller than traditional register-based estimates even after conditioning on demographics and job history, and helps explain why many hold few liquid assets.

Data: Income and employment registers (IND, IDA), Wealth registers (FORM), Custom survey matched on PNR

Disaggregated Economic Accounts

Asger Lau Andersen, Kilian Huber, Niels Johannesen, Ludwig Straub and Emil Toft Vestergaard · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2026 · Individual + firm

Builds a system of national accounts broken down into bilateral money flows between region-by-industry consumer cells, producer cells, the government and the rest of the world, and implements it for Denmark using bank transaction data and registers. Spending is locally and urban biased, and fiscal transfers raise GDP more when targeted at cells whose spending reaches cells with high unemployment.

Data: Geocoded transaction data from Danske Bank, Employment and residence registers (IDA, BEF), Firm accounts and VAT data (FIRE, VNDS), National accounts

Beliefs About the Economy Are Excessively Sensitive to Household-Level Shocks: Evidence from Linked Survey and Administrative Data

Dmitry Taubinsky, Luigi Butera, Matteo Saccarola and Chen Lian · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2026 · Individual

Links Statistics Denmark's consumer expectations survey to the registers to show that people's inflation forecasts and backcasts move strongly, and negatively, with their own recent and expected income changes, far more than actual inflation does. The pattern departs from Bayesian rational expectations and is consistent with affect-cued recall, where bad personal events cue pessimistic recollections.

Data: Consumer expectations survey linked on PNR, Income registers (IND), Health event records (LPR)

Uses sudden first parental deaths and matched controls in Danish administrative data. The Direct and Indirect Effects of Online Job Search Advice

A large two-stage randomised experiment on the Danish public employment service's online platform gave job seekers vacancy information and occupational recommendations, with treatment intensity varying by region. Advice raises employment and earnings when few job seekers receive it, but the gains vanish at high intensity because treated job seekers crowd into the same occupations.

Fertility and Family Labor Supply

Thomas H. Jørgensen, Katrine Jakobsen and Hamish Low · American Economic Review (forthcoming), 2026 · Individual

Uses the 2009 Danish tax reform to show that higher net-of-tax wages for women reduce fertility while higher wages for men raise it. Data: Income tax records and tax reform variation, Birth and family links (BEF), Employment and wages (IDA, BFL)

Changing Tracks: Human Capital Investment after Loss of Ability

Anders Humlum, Jakob R. Munch and Pernille Plato · American Economic Review, 2025 · Individual

Uses quasi-random work accidents in Danish administrative data to show that injured workers enrol in bachelor's programmes that build on their previous training. Builds a model in which effort today raises earnings tomorrow, so responses to taxes are delayed and mediated by job switches. Links a large survey to income registers to compare perceived and actual positions in the income distribution and within reference groups (co-workers, neighbours, schoolmates). Estimates a flexible model of worker and firm heterogeneity in wages and job mobility on Danish matched employer-employee data. Workers and firms sort positively, with sorting operating through both wages and non-wage amenities, and worker heterogeneity accounts for the bulk of wage dispersion.

Reference Dependence in the Housing Market

Steffen Andersen, Cristian Badarinza, Lu Liu, Julie Marx and Tarun Ramadorai · American Economic Review, 2022 · Individual

Combines Danish housing listings and transactions with register data on owners to estimate a structural model of listing and sale decisions with loss aversion and financial constraints. Households derive utility from gains over the original purchase price and weigh losses about 2.5 times more than gains, which helps explain the co-movement of house prices and turnover.

The Returns to Nursing: Evidence from a Parental-Leave Program

Benjamin U. Friedrich and Martin B. Hackmann · Review of Economic Studies, 2021 · Individual + firm

A Danish parental-leave programme offering up to a year off per child aged 0 to 8 induced a nurse shortage that hospitals and nursing homes could not replace. Nurse employment fell by 15 percent in hospitals and 10 percent in nursing homes, 30-day readmissions of heart attack patients rose, and nursing home mortality increased sharply.

Combines a Roy model, search frictions, compensating differentials and on-the-job human capital accumulation in one framework, establishes what is non-parametrically identified, and estimates it on Danish matched data. Links incentivised time-discounting experiments to the wealth positions of a large sample of middle-aged Danes in the registers. A randomised field experiment with the Danish tax authority on about 40,000 taxpayers. Breaking Bad: How Health Shocks Prompt Crime

Steffen Andersen, Elin Colmsjö, Gianpaolo Parise and Kim Peijnenburg · AEJ: Applied Economics, 2026 · Individual

Uses plausibly exogenous timing of cancer diagnoses in Danish registers to show that a health shock raises the probability of committing a crime, both first offences and re-offences, persistently. Patients replace lost legal income with illegal income and face a lower expected cost of punishment because of reduced survival, and welfare programmes that cushion the income loss dampen the effect.

Healthy at Work? Evidence from a Social Experimental Evaluation of a Firm-Based Wellness Program

Marianne Simonsen and Lars Skipper · AEJ: Economic Policy, 2025 · Individual + firm
