Taxation and public finance

Behavioural responses to income, wealth and audit policy, identified from reforms and experiments on full-population tax records.

Taxing the Wealth of the Poor: Evidence from the Danish Old-Age Support Asset Test

Niels Johannesen, Johan Sæverud and Emmanuel Saez · AER: Insights, 2026 · Individual

The asset test for Danish old-age support creates diffuse but large excess mass in liquid assets just below the threshold among the eligible, relative to comparable ineligible groups. Cohort analysis suggests the test reduces liquid assets by about 20 percent near the threshold, and high-frequency bank data show the responses are permanent rather than timed around the test date.

Data: Wealth registers (FORM), Pension and benefit records, High-frequency bank account data

Fertility and Family Labor Supply

Thomas H. Jørgensen, Katrine Jakobsen and Hamish Low · American Economic Review (forthcoming), 2026 · Individual

Uses the 2009 Danish tax reform to show that higher net-of-tax wages for women reduce fertility while higher wages for men raise it. A life-cycle model of couples choosing the timing and number of children, estimated on register data, implies that allowing fertility to adjust raises women's labour supply responsiveness substantially and that human capital depreciation around births drives much of the long-run gender wage gap.

Data: Income tax records and tax reform variation, Birth and family links (BEF), Employment and wages (IDA, BFL)

Micro versus Macro Labor Supply Elasticities: The Role of Dynamic Returns to Effort

Henrik Kleven, Claus Thustrup Kreiner, Kristian Larsen and Jakob Egholt Søgaard · American Economic Review, 2025 · Individual

Builds a model in which effort today raises earnings tomorrow, so responses to taxes are delayed and mediated by job switches. Danish administrative data confirm the model's life-cycle predictions for earnings and hours, and a quasi-experimental design based on job switchers finds a long-run earnings elasticity around 0.5, against a short-run elasticity of about 0.2.

Data: Income tax records (IND), Hours and job spells from payroll data (BFL) and IDA

Public Pensions and Private Savings

Esteban García-Miralles and Jonathan M. Leganza · AEJ: Economic Policy, 2024 · Individual

A Danish reform raising the social security eligibility age, studied with a regression discontinuity in administrative data, increased contributions to personal and employer pension accounts when later eligibility kept people working longer. Inertia in previous savings behaviour and firms' default contribution rates are the key mechanisms.

Data: Pension contribution records, Income and employment registers, Wealth registers (FORM)

New Evidence on Welfare's Disincentive for the Youth Using Administrative Panel Data

Olivier B. Bargain and Anders B. Jonassen · Review of Economics and Statistics, 2024 · Individual

Maximum social assistance for childless unmarried Danes rose by 55 percent at age 25 between 2000 and 2006. Only the low-skilled respond: employment falls by 2 to 3 percent and benefit take-up rises by 10 to 14 percent, two thirds through moves from work to welfare, and crime shows no discontinuity.

Data: Benefit registers (DREAM), Monthly employment and earnings, Education and crime registers

Do People Respond to the Mortgage Interest Deduction? Quasi-Experimental Evidence from Denmark

Jonathan Gruber, Amalie Sofie Jensen and Henrik Kleven · AEJ: Economic Policy, 2021 · Individual

A major reform that scaled back the mortgage interest deduction for middle- and high-income households had a precisely estimated zero effect on home ownership. It did reduce the size and price of houses bought, lowered household indebtedness and pushed down equilibrium house prices.

Data: Income tax records with interest deductions, Property registers and transaction prices, Mortgage debt from wealth registers (FORM)

Wealth Taxation and Wealth Accumulation: Theory and Evidence from Denmark

Katrine Jakobsen, Kristian Jakobsen, Henrik Kleven and Gabriel Zucman · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2020 · Individual

Exploits the 1989 Danish wealth tax reform, which cut rates sharply for the wealthy, to estimate long-run effects of wealth taxes on wealth accumulation. Responses are sizable for the very wealthy (top one percent) and small for the moderately wealthy, with effects that keep growing over a decade.

Data: Wealth tax records 1980–1996 (third-party reported assets and debt), Income tax records

The Welfare Magnet Hypothesis: Evidence from an Immigrant Welfare Scheme in Denmark

Ole Agersnap, Amalie Sofie Jensen and Henrik Kleven · AER: Insights, 2020 · Individual

The 2002 cut in benefits for non-EU immigrants, its repeal and its reintroduction provide repeated quasi-experiments on the welfare magnet hypothesis. The cut reduced net immigration by about 5,000 people a year and the repeal reversed it almost exactly, implying a migration elasticity with respect to benefits of 1.3.

Data: Population and migration registers (BEF), Benefit registers (DREAM)

Tax Evasion and Inequality

Annette Alstadsæter, Niels Johannesen and Gabriel Zucman · American Economic Review, 2019 · Individual

Matches leaked offshore account data (HSBC Switzerland, Panama Papers) and tax amnesty records to Scandinavian wealth registers. Tax evasion rises steeply with wealth: the top 0.01 percent evade about a quarter of their taxes, which materially raises measured top wealth shares.

Data: Danish, Norwegian and Swedish wealth and income tax records, Leaked offshore account lists, Tax amnesty disclosures

Liquidity Constraint Tightness and Consumer Responses to Fiscal Stimulus Policy

Claus Thustrup Kreiner, David Dreyer Lassen and Søren Leth-Petersen · AEJ: Economic Policy, 2019 · Individual

The 2009 payout of Special Pension savings worked as a fiscal stimulus. Measuring constraint tightness by the marginal interest rate households pay, the spending response is highest for households facing high marginal rates and otherwise small, which supports targeting stimulus at the credit constrained.

Data: Tax records on interest payments and loans, Survey of spending responses linked to registers, Pension payout records

Tax Reforms and Intertemporal Shifting of Wage Income: Evidence from Danish Monthly Payroll Records

Claus Thustrup Kreiner, Søren Leth-Petersen and Peer Ebbesen Skov · AEJ: Economic Policy, 2016 · Individual + firm

The 2010 cut in the top marginal tax rate was announced in advance, and monthly payroll data show top earners shifting wage income from December 2009 into January 2010. Shifting is concentrated among managers and employees of small firms, and it inflates short-run elasticity estimates.

Data: Monthly payroll records from eIndkomst 2008–2010, Employer characteristics (firm size)

Do Employer Pension Contributions Reflect Employee Preferences? Evidence from a Retirement Savings Reform in Denmark

Itzik Fadlon, Jessica Laird and Torben Heien Nielsen · AEJ: Applied Economics, 2016 · Individual + firm

A reform that cut the subsidy to capital pension accounts for top-bracket workers led employers to reduce contributions to those accounts immediately, by more the larger the share of affected employees, and to shift the money to annuity accounts. Employer contributions track employees' savings preferences.

Data: Pension contribution records from tax data, Employer links (IDA), Income tax records

Estimating Taxable Income Responses Using Danish Tax Reforms

Henrik Jacobsen Kleven and Esben Anton Schultz · AEJ: Economic Policy, 2014 · Individual

Uses a series of Danish tax reforms between 1984 and 2005 and full-population tax records to estimate the elasticity of taxable income. Elasticities are small for labour income (around 0.05) and larger for capital income and for top earners, and they are larger for big reforms than small ones.

Data: Full-population income tax records 1984–2005, Tax schedule parameters by year

Migration and Wage Effects of Taxing Top Earners: Evidence from the Foreigners' Tax Scheme in Denmark

Henrik Jacobsen Kleven, Camille Landais, Emmanuel Saez and Esben Schultz · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2014 · Individual

The 1991 preferential tax scheme for high-earning foreigners roughly doubled the number of highly paid foreigners in Denmark. The implied elasticity of migration with respect to the net-of-tax rate is above one, far larger than domestic labour supply elasticities, and the scheme generated modest wage spillovers.

Data: Full-population income tax records, Migration and residence histories (BEF), Employer links (IDA)

Active vs. Passive Decisions and Crowd-Out in Retirement Savings Accounts: Evidence from Denmark

Raj Chetty, John N. Friedman, Søren Leth-Petersen, Torben Heien Nielsen and Tore Olsen · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2014 · Individual

Uses the universe of Danish savings records to show that tax subsidies for retirement accounts mostly shift saving between accounts: each krone of subsidy raises total saving by about one øre. Automatic employer contributions, by contrast, raise total saving almost one for one, because roughly 85 percent of individuals are passive savers who do not re-optimise.

Data: Income tax records with pension contributions, Third-party reported wealth (assets and liabilities), Employer links (IDA)

Unwilling or Unable to Cheat? Evidence from a Tax Audit Experiment in Denmark

Henrik Jacobsen Kleven, Martin B. Knudsen, Claus Thustrup Kreiner, Søren Pedersen and Emmanuel Saez · Econometrica, 2011 · Individual

A randomised field experiment with the Danish tax authority on about 40,000 taxpayers. Evasion is close to zero for third-party reported income but substantial for self-reported income, and audit threat letters raise reported self-reported income. Information, not morality, is what makes modern tax systems work.

Data: Individual tax returns, Audit outcomes from the SKAT experiment, Third-party information reports

Adjustment Costs, Firm Responses, and Micro vs. Macro Labor Supply Elasticities: Evidence from Danish Tax Records

Raj Chetty, John N. Friedman, Tore Olsen and Luigi Pistaferri · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2011 · Individual + firm

Uses bunching at kinks in the Danish tax schedule to show that observed earnings responses are shaped by search costs and by firms coordinating hours around tax thresholds. Once adjustment frictions are accounted for, the structural labour supply elasticity is around 0.3, far above the small elasticities from individual-level micro studies, which helps reconcile micro and macro estimates.

Data: Full-population income tax records 1994–2001, Employer links (IDA) and occupation codes

Household finance, saving and consumption

Saving, borrowing, consumption responses and wealth dynamics, built on third-party reported assets, debt and imputed consumption.

Subjective Earnings Risk

Andrew Caplin, Victoria Gregory, Eungik Lee, Søren Leth-Petersen and Johan Sæverud · Review of Economic Studies, 2026 · Individual

Introduces a survey instrument for earnings risk that allows for quitting and being fired, fielded to Danish workers and linked to their register earnings histories. Job transitions drive subjective risk, which is many times smaller than traditional register-based estimates even after conditioning on demographics and job history, and helps explain why many hold few liquid assets.

Data: Income and employment registers (IND, IDA), Wealth registers (FORM), Custom survey matched on PNR

Beliefs About the Economy Are Excessively Sensitive to Household-Level Shocks: Evidence from Linked Survey and Administrative Data

Dmitry Taubinsky, Luigi Butera, Matteo Saccarola and Chen Lian · Quarterly Journal of Economics, 2026 · Individual

Links Statistics Denmark's consumer expectations survey to the registers to show that people's inflation forecasts and backcasts move strongly, and negatively, with their own recent and expected income changes, far more than actual inflation does. The pattern departs from Bayesian rational expectations and is consistent with affect-cued recall, where bad personal events cue pessimistic recollections.

Data: Consumer expectations survey linked on PNR, Income registers (IND), Health event records (LPR)

Survivors' Mental Health and the Protective Role of Income Stability

Itzik Fadlon, Astrid Sophie Fugleholm and Torben Heien Nielsen · AER: Insights, 2026 · Individual

Documents the immediate, large and lingering rise in mental health medication after a spouse's death across the universe of Danish households. A large share of the spike is explained by the income loss the death imposes, so safety-net liquidity protects survivors' mental health.

Data: Prescription register (LMDB), Death register, Income and pension registers, Family links (BEF)

Asger Lau Andersen, Niels Johannesen and Adam Sheridan · AER: Insights, 2024 · Individual

Uses lottery-like variation in stock market gains across investors with similar portfolios, observed in granular bank data, to trace spending responses. Responses are immediate and persistent, cumulating to a marginal propensity to consume of 4.4 percent over one year and 16 percent over three, consistent with the permanent income hypothesis with some inattention.

Data: Account-level spending and stock portfolios from Danske Bank, Register demographics and income

How Do Households Respond to Job Loss? Lessons from Multiple High-Frequency Datasets

Asger Lau Andersen, Amalie Sofie Jensen, Niels Johannesen, Claus Thustrup Kreiner, Søren Leth-Petersen and Adam Sheridan · AEJ: Applied Economics, 2023 · Individual

Over two years households cut spending by 30 percent of the income loss and cover about half of it by running down liquid balances; spousal labour supply, private transfers, home equity extraction and consumer credit contribute less.

Data: Account-level spending, balances and loans from Danske Bank, Unemployment and income registers (DREAM, IND), Family links (BEF)

Long-Run Saving Dynamics: Evidence from Unexpected Inheritances

Jeppe Druedahl and Alessandro Martinello · Review of Economics and Statistics, 2022 · Individual

Sudden parental deaths deliver unexpected inheritances. Heirs' wealth jumps and then declines over roughly a decade back toward its pre-inheritance path, which implies a moderate marginal propensity to consume out of wealth and informs the calibration of buffer-stock saving models.

Data: Wealth registers (FORM), Death register with cause of death, Family links (BEF), Income registers

Financial Trouble Across Generations: Evidence from the Universe of Personal Loans in Denmark

Claus Thustrup Kreiner, Søren Leth-Petersen and Louise Charlotte Willerslev-Olsen · Economic Journal, 2020 · Individual

Loan-level records on all personal loans show strong intergenerational persistence in financial trouble: children of parents who default are far more likely to default themselves, and the correlation is present already in early adulthood and is not explained by income or education.

Data: Universe of personal loans reported to the tax authority (loan-level, with default status), Family links (BEF), Income and education registers

Sources of Inaction in Household Finance: Evidence from the Danish Mortgage Market

Steffen Andersen, John Y. Campbell, Kasper Meisner Nielsen and Tarun Ramadorai · American Economic Review, 2020 · Individual

Many Danish households fail to refinance fixed-rate mortgages when it is clearly profitable. A model with inattention and psychological refinancing costs fits the data, and both frictions are stronger for households with less education, income and financial wealth.

Data: Loan-level mortgage data from Danish mortgage banks, Income, wealth, education and demographics from registers

Time Discounting and Wealth Inequality

Thomas Epper, Ernst Fehr, Helga Fehr-Duda, Claus Thustrup Kreiner, David Dreyer Lassen, Søren Leth-Petersen and Gregers Nytoft Rasmussen · American Economic Review, 2020 · Individual

Links incentivised time-discounting experiments to the wealth positions of a large sample of middle-aged Danes in the registers. Patient individuals sit markedly higher in the wealth distribution, and the association survives controls for education, income profiles, school grades, initial and parental wealth, credit constraints and risk preferences, which points to saving behaviour as the channel.

Data: Wealth registers (FORM), Income, education and family registers, Incentivised online experiment linked on PNR

Born with a Silver Spoon? Danish Evidence on Wealth Inequality in Childhood

Simon Halphen Boserup, Wojciech Kopczuk and Claus Thustrup Kreiner · Economic Journal, 2018 · Individual

Third-party reported wealth records of children show that wealth inequality is as high in childhood as among adults, with top shares driven by transfers from parents and grandparents. Wealth at age 18 predicts wealth in adulthood, and intergenerational wealth correlations are present from early ages.

Data: Wealth registers (FORM) for children and parents, Family links (BEF), Income registers

The Consumption Effects of the 2007–2008 Financial Crisis: Evidence from Households in Denmark

Thais Lærkholm Jensen and Niels Johannesen · American Economic Review, 2017 · Individual

Customers of banks that were hit hard by the crisis cut consumption more than customers of healthier banks. The channel is reduced credit availability: exposed households borrowed less and spent less, which shows how bank distress transmits to the real economy through households.

Data: Customer-bank links from tax reports on accounts and loans, Bank balance sheets, Imputed consumption from income and wealth registers

Housing Wealth and Consumption: A Micro Panel Study

Martin Browning, Mette Gørtz and Søren Leth-Petersen · Economic Journal, 2013 · Individual

Uses register-based imputed consumption and house price changes over the 1987 to 1996 boom and bust to test whether housing wealth drives spending. For most households consumption does not respond to unanticipated house price changes; the response is confined to young households, consistent with a collateral rather than a wealth effect.

Data: Income and wealth tax records with property values, Consumption imputed from income and wealth changes

Intertemporal Consumption and Credit Constraints: Does Total Expenditure Respond to an Exogenous Shock to Credit?

Søren Leth-Petersen · American Economic Review, 2010 · Individual

The 1992 reform that let Danish homeowners borrow against housing equity provides an exogenous credit shock. Total expenditure responded only modestly on average, with the response concentrated among young households with little liquid wealth, which indicates that credit constraints bind for a minority.

Data: Income and wealth tax records, Consumption imputed from income minus change in wealth, Property values

Imputing Consumption from Income and Wealth Information

Martin Browning and Søren Leth-Petersen · Economic Journal, 2003 · Individual

Tests whether consumption can be imputed from register data as income minus the change in wealth, by comparing the imputation with expenditure survey responses for the same households. The imputation works well for most households but is noisy for those with housing transactions or large stock holdings.

Data: Income and wealth tax records, Danish Expenditure Survey linked to registers

Labour markets, wages and sorting

Search and matching, wage dynamics and who works with whom, using matched employer-employee data.

Reskilling and Resilience

Anders Humlum and Pernille Plato · AER: Insights (forthcoming), 2026 · Individual

Studies how retraining shapes workers' resilience to labour market shocks in Danish administrative data, extending the authors' work on human capital investment after injury to the question of which workers reskill and how reskilling protects earnings when jobs disappear.

Data: Education and training registers, Employment and earnings registers (IDA, BFL, IND)

Sarah Sander · Economic Journal, 2026 · Individual

Exploits the roll-out of daycare centres across Danish municipalities to follow mothers over their working lives. Daycare availability raises participation, hours and earnings sixteen years after the first birth, with effects driven by low-educated mothers and mediated partly through fertility and separation.

Data: Historical daycare roll-out by municipality, Employment and earnings registers (IDA, IND), Birth and family links (BEF)

Anders Humlum, Jakob R. Munch and Pernille Plato · American Economic Review, 2025 · Individual

Uses quasi-random work accidents in Danish administrative data to show that injured workers enrol in bachelor's programmes that build on their previous training. Exploiting differences in how stackable degrees are, higher education moves injured workers from disability benefits into full-time jobs paying 25 percent more than before injury, and reskilling subsidies pay for themselves four times over.

Data: Work accident records linked to the National Patient Register, Education enrolment registers (UDDA, KOTRE), Income, employment and disability benefit registers (IND, IDA, DREAM)

N. Meltem Daysal, William N. Evans, Mikkel Hasse Pedersen and Mircea Trandafir · AEJ: Economic Policy, 2025 · Individual

A Danish guideline change shifted which breast cancer patients received radiation therapy, giving quasi-experimental variation in treatment. Radiotherapy reproduces the survival gains found in trials and has economic returns: ten years after diagnosis it raises employment by 37 percent and earnings by 45 percent, undoing most of the post-diagnosis employment drop.

Data: Cancer registry and treatment records, National Patient Register, Income and employment registers

Estimates a flexible model of worker and firm heterogeneity in wages and job mobility on Danish matched employer-employee data. Workers and firms sort positively, with sorting operating through both wages and non-wage amenities, and worker heterogeneity accounts for the bulk of wage dispersion.

Data: Matched employer-employee data (IDA), Annual earnings and job spells

The Returns to Nursing: Evidence from a Parental-Leave Program

Benjamin U. Friedrich and Martin B. Hackmann · Review of Economic Studies, 2021 · Individual + firm

A Danish parental-leave programme offering up to a year off per child aged 0 to 8 induced a nurse shortage that hospitals and nursing homes could not replace. Nurse employment fell by 15 percent in hospitals and 10 percent in nursing homes, 30-day readmissions of heart attack patients rose, and nursing home mortality increased sharply.

Data: Matched employer-employee data (IDA) for health workers, National Patient Register, Death register, Nursing home residence

Does Biology Drive Child Penalties? Evidence from Biological and Adoptive Families

Henrik Kleven, Camille Landais and Jakob Egholt Søgaard · AER: Insights, 2021 · Individual

Compares event-study child penalties for biological and adoptive mothers using almost 40 years of Danish adoption data. Short-run penalties are slightly larger for biological mothers, but long-run penalties are virtually identical, so biology is not a key driver of child-related gender gaps.

Data: Adoption records linked to BEF, Income and employment registers 1980–2017

Family Labor Supply Responses to Severe Health Shocks: Evidence from Danish Administrative Records

Itzik Fadlon and Torben Heien Nielsen · AEJ: Applied Economics, 2021 · Individual

Compares households hit by a fatal versus a non-fatal health shock at the same time. After a spouse's death the surviving spouse raises labour supply to self-insure, while after a non-fatal shock spouses reduce labour supply to care for the patient. Children of the household adjust less.

Data: National Patient Register (heart attacks, strokes), Death register, Income and employment registers, Family links (BEF)

Estimation of a Roy/Search/Compensating Differential Model of the Labor Market

Christopher Taber and Rune Vejlin · Econometrica, 2020 · Individual + firm

Combines a Roy model, search frictions, compensating differentials and on-the-job human capital accumulation in one framework, establishes what is non-parametrically identified, and estimates it on Danish matched data. Pre-market skills account for most wage variation, while preferences for non-wage job attributes and search frictions matter greatly for turnover and for which jobs workers choose.

Data: Matched employer-employee data (IDA), Job spells and hourly wages

Do Lower Minimum Wages for Young Workers Raise Their Employment? Evidence from a Danish Discontinuity

Claus Thustrup Kreiner, Daniel Reck and Peer Ebbesen Skov · Review of Economics and Statistics, 2020 · Individual

Danish minimum wages jump by 40 percent at age 18. Monthly payroll records for the whole population show employment falling by 33 percent and hours by 45 percent at the discontinuity, leaving total wage payments almost unchanged, with an implied employment elasticity between 0.6 and 1.1.

Data: Monthly payroll records (BFL) with hours and wages, Population register (BEF) for exact birth dates

Children and Gender Inequality: Evidence from Denmark

Henrik Kleven, Camille Landais and Jakob Egholt Søgaard · AEJ: Applied Economics, 2019 · Individual

Event studies around the birth of the first child show mothers' earnings fall about 20 percent relative to fathers' in the long run, through hours, participation and wage rates. These child penalties now account for most of the remaining gender earnings gap and are transmitted across generations through mothers' own upbringing.

Data: Income and employment registers 1980–2013 (IDA, IND), Birth and family links (BEF), Hours and occupation

An Empirical Model of Wage Dispersion with Sorting

Jesper Bagger and Rasmus Lentz · Review of Economic Studies, 2019 · Individual + firm

Estimates an equilibrium search model with endogenous search intensity and two-sided heterogeneity on Danish matched data. Finds positive assortative matching between worker and firm productivity, and quantifies how much of wage dispersion is due to sorting versus search frictions.

Subjective Earnings Risk

Andrew Caplin, Victoria Gregory, Eungik Lee, Søren Leth-Petersen and Johan Sæverud · Review of Economic Studies, 2026 · Individual

Introduces a survey instrument for earnings risk that allows for quitting and being fired, fielded to Danish workers and linked to their register earnings histories. Job transitions drive subjective risk, which is many times smaller than traditional register-based estimates even after conditioning on demographics and job history, and helps explain why many hold few liquid assets.

Data: Income and employment registers (IND, IDA), Wealth registers (FORM), Custom survey matched on PNR

Imputing Consumption from Income and Wealth Information

Martin Browning and Søren Leth-Petersen · Economic Journal, 2003 · Individual

Tests whether consumption can be imputed from register data as income minus the change in wealth, by comparing the imputation with expenditure survey responses for the same households. The imputation works well for most households but is noisy for those with housing transactions or large stock holdings.

Data: Income and wealth tax records, Danish Expenditure Survey linked to registers

Product innovation and growth at the firm level.

An Empirical Model of Growth Through Product Innovation

Rasmus Lentz and Dale T. Mortensen · Econometrica, 2008 · Firm

Estimates a Schumpeterian model of firm growth through product innovation on a Danish firm panel. Reallocation of labour from less to more innovative firms accounts for roughly half of aggregate productivity growth, with the remainder coming from innovation within firms.

Data: Firm panel from the Accounts Statistics (value added, employment, capital) 1992–1997
