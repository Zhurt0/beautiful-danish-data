Who paid for the research

An acknowledgment was found in 155 of the 169 papers whose full text could be read: 47 Top 5 papers and 108 in the other journals. The two Danish research councils are named most often, and at similar rates in both groups. What sets the Top 5 papers apart is American money: US foundations and the US National Science Foundation are named far more often there.

Who is thanked

The acknowledgments thank 608 different people for comments, discussions or help. Top 5 papers thank more of them, 9.3 on average against 4.9 in the other journals, and they more often thank someone highly cited: 28 of 47 Top 5 papers (60%) against 32 of 108 others (30%).

4.9people thanked by the average paper in other journals
70of the people thanked are highly cited

Thanking a highly cited economist is not what gets a paper into the Top 5. In a regression that holds fixed where the authors are based, whether one is at a leading department, the sample size and the year, the difference shrinks to 9 percentage points and is no longer distinguishable from zero (p = 0.31). Teams with authors at leading departments both reach the Top 5 more often and know more of these people.

Names were read from the acknowledgment by a script and matched by first initial and surname, so two people who share both are merged and a name split across lines can be missed. Most of the texts read are working-paper versions.

Who is thanked together

The 48 people in the chart are each thanked in at least three papers. A line joins two people who are thanked together in at least two papers, and people who are thanked together often sit close to each other. Two groups stand out: economists in Copenhagen around Søren Leth-Petersen and Claus Thustrup Kreiner, and public-finance economists in the United States around Raj Chetty and Emmanuel Saez.
