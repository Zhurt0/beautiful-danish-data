Survey

Many papers link register data to a survey through the personal identifier: the Labour Force Survey for hours and occupations, the Expenditure Survey to validate imputed consumption, children's cohort surveys, election studies, or a survey the authors fielded themselves. This page collects the questionnaires behind those surveys, so you can see exactly what respondents were asked.

23 surveys used by 29 papers; 19 have question wording online: a full questionnaire, a codebook with the questions, an appendix listing them, or at least the questions quoted in the paper (44 documents in all). Every document was opened and checked before it was listed. Links point to the original publisher, so the files stay with their owners and their reuse terms; the microdata themselves are confidential and accessed through Statistics Denmark or the survey owner.

Official and academic surveys 17

Accounts Statistics for the Non-Agricultural Private Sector (Regnskabsstatistik for private byerhverv), firm accounts survey

Statistics Denmark (Danmarks Statistik) · Survey home page

Annual mandatory survey of a sample of firms in the non-agricultural private sector (all large firms plus a size-stratified sample of smaller ones, roughly 8,000-9,000 firms around 1999-2003) reporting detailed income statement, balance sheet, investment and employment/hours items; combined with tax-register data for non-sampled firms.

Used by: Bagger et al. (2014) · Waves used: 1999–2003

  • Questionnaire Regnskabsstatistik 2025 - kladde (draft of the full online reporting form: all questions) Danish · 2025 · 10 pages

Access: Firm-level microdata (FIRE/REGN registers) via Statistics Denmark researcher access (forskeradgang), linkable to IDA/employer-employee data.

Only the current (2025) reporting form is online; no questionnaire for the 1999-2003 waves used by Bagger et al. (2014) was found. The 2025 draft's question text is embedded as vector graphics (not extractable text) but was confirmed visually (e.g. p.3: Resultatopgørelse items 1-6). Reporter page with XBRL template: https://www.dst.dk/da/Indberet/oplysningssider/regnskabsstatistik. Bagger et al. describe the data in Section 4 and Appendix B (sampling scheme) of the UCL working-paper version.

Children and Youth in Denmark (CYD) / Børn og unge i Danmark - velfærd og trivsel

SFI - Danish National Centre for Social Research, now VIVE (funded by Bikubenfonden) · Survey home page

Repeated cross-section of random samples of Danish children aged about 3, 7, 11, 15 and 19 (2009 and 2013; later rounds 2017/2018); parents of the 3- and 7-year-olds and the older children themselves answer by web with telephone follow-up (11-year-olds by personal interview), linked to Statistics Denmark registers.

Used by: Rossin-Slater and Wüst (2018) · Waves used: as used by Rossin-Slater and Wüst (2018)

  • Documentation Børn og unge i Danmark - velfærd og trivsel 2010 (SFI report on the 2009 survey) Danish · 2009 · 258 pages · Appendix Bilag 1, pp. 248-253: list of indicators by domain and age group (indicator descriptions, not full question wording)
  • Documentation Børn og unge i Danmark - velfærd og trivsel 2014 (SFI report on the 2013 survey) Danish · 2013 · 247 pages · Introduction 'Undersøgelsens datagrund og metoder' (pp. ~21-27) describes the questionnaire design and modes; Bilag 1 (methods)

Access: Survey microdata are held by VIVE; researchers gain access through a VIVE agreement and analyse them linked to registers on Statistics Denmark's research servers.

Rossin-Slater and Wüst use the 2009 CYD wave (custody arrangements), pooled with DALSC 2007 (Appendix C). No questionnaire PDF or codebook found online for 2009 or 2013. The paper's source link (sfi.dk/children_and_young_people_in_denmark-7395.aspx) is defunct and the Wayback Machine could not be reached. No Danish Data Archive study page found.

Copenhagen Life Panel (CLP)

Copenhagen Life Panel research team, Department of Economics / CEBI, University of Copenhagen

Online panel survey of a random sample of adults drawn from the Danish population register, fielded every January since 2020 and linkable to Statistics Denmark administrative registers; the January 2021 wave carried the conditional earnings-expectations instrument of Caplin et al.

Used by: Caplin et al. (2026) · Waves used: January 2021 wave

  • Questions in appendix Online Appendix for 'Subjective Earnings Risk' (Caplin, Gregory, Lee, Leth-Petersen, Sæverud), Appendix A: Survey questions (CLP January 2021 earnings-expectations module, with variable names and routing) English · January 2021 · 25 pages · Appendix A, pp. 1-6
  • Documentation Subjective Earnings Risk, ReStud accepted manuscript (Section 2 describes the instrument and the CLP; Figure 1 instrument overview; states questionnaire is in Online Appendix A) English · January 2021 · 44 pages · Section 2, pp. 5-10

Access: Survey data are held by the CLP team on Statistics Denmark's research servers and linked to registers; access by arrangement with the CLP researchers via a DST Research Service project.

No public CLP project website, full-panel questionnaire or codebook was found; only the earnings-risk module (in the St. Louis Fed WP 2023-003 online appendix, dated April 2025) is documented. The ReStud online appendix itself was not located; the restud.com paper page links no supplementary files.

Danish Consumer Expectations Survey / Consumer Confidence Survey (Forbrugerforventningsundersøgelsen, Forbrugerforventninger; part of Statistics Denmark's Omnibus survey)

Statistics Denmark (Danmarks Statistik), within the EU Joint Harmonised Programme of Business and Consumer Surveys (European Commission DG ECFIN) · Survey home page

Monthly repeated cross-section of about 1,500 residents aged 16-74 drawn from the CPR register, interviewed by telephone/web as the first module of Statistics Denmark's Omnibus survey; questions are harmonised with the European Commission's consumer survey and include quantitative inflation perceptions and expectations.

Used by: Taubinsky et al. (2026) · Waves used: as used by Taubinsky et al.

Access: Microdata linked to registers via Statistics Denmark researcher access (forskeradgang); aggregate series in Statistikbanken (FORV1 etc.).

Danish Expenditure Survey / Household Budget Survey (Forbrugsundersøgelsen)

Statistics Denmark (Danmarks Statistik) · Survey home page

Annual (since 1994) sample survey of private households combining a face-to-face/CAPI interview on fixed and large expenditures over the past 12 months with a 14-day expenditure diary (regnskabshæfte), supplemented with register data; results are usually published as pooled three-year samples.

Used by: Browning and Leth-Petersen (2003), Giorgi et al. (2019), Leth-Petersen (2010), Eshaghnia et al. (2025) · Waves used: 1994–1996

  • Documentation Forbrugsundersøgelsen - Metodebeskrivelse: Fra dataindsamling til offentliggørelse (Danmarks Statistik 1999; describes the post-1994 design, interview topics, diaries, coding and weighting; Bilag lists fieldwork materials) Danish · 1994 onwards (1995-1997 data) · 84 pages
  • Documentation Statistikdokumentation: Forbrugsundersøgelsen (current documentation; yearly PDFs for 2013-2024) Danish · 2013-2024

Access: Microdata via Statistics Denmark's Research Service (register-linkable household files); no public-use file.

No interview questionnaire (interviewskema) or expenditure diary for 1994-1996, or for any later year, was found online at dst.dk, nor a DDA/Rigsarkivet study page; the 1999 method description describes the interview content but does not reproduce question wording. The papers using it do not reproduce questions.

Danish Health and Morbidity Survey 2000 / Danish National Health Interview Survey (Sundheds- og sygelighedsundersøgelsen 2000, SUSY 2000)

Danish Time Use Survey 2001 (DTUS; Danish: Tidsanvendelsesundersøgelsen 2001 / Danskernes tidsanvendelse 2001)

SFI - Danish National Institute of Social Research (now VIVE), with Rockwool Foundation Research Unit; fieldwork by SFI-Survey · Survey home page

Nationally representative survey of 2,739 Danes aged 16-74 (2001; split sample spring/autumn): CAPI home interview with a questionnaire on background, normal weekly paid and household work hours, personal expenditures and well-being, plus self-completed 10-minute time diaries for one weekday and one weekend day by respondent and partner (6,624 diaries); linked to Statistics Denmark registers. Repeat of the 1987 survey; followed by the 2008/09 Danish Time Use and Consumption Survey.

Used by: Foerster (2024) · Waves used: 2001

  • Questions quoted in paper Browning & Gørtz (2006), Spending Time and Money within the Household, Oxford Economics Discussion Paper 288 (the data description Foerster cites, fn. 18): quotes the DTUS 2001 personal-expenditure questions and describes the normal-hours housework/market-work questions English · 2001 · 43 pages · Section 2.2 'Time use' (PDF p. 6, housework/market-work hours question described) and Section 2.3 'Personal expenditures' (PDF p. 9, printed p. 8: expenditure question wording quoted with item list and footnotes 5-6)
  • Documentation Bonke (2002), Paid work and unpaid work: diary information versus questionnaire information, SFI Working Paper 26:2002 - describes the DTUS 2001 questionnaire items on paid work and the 9 household-work tasks and the diary design English · 2001 · 21 pages · Tables 1-2 and text, PDF pp. 6-8
  • Documentation Bonke (2002), Tid og velfærd, SFI rapport 02:26 - main report; Kapitel 6 'Data og bortfald' lists questionnaire and diary contents, sampling and non-response Danish · 2001 · 112 pages · Section 1.4 'Undersøgelsesmetode' (PDF pp. 18-19) and Kapitel 6 (PDF pp. 87-92)

Access: Microdata held by VIVE (formerly SFI) / Rockwool Foundation; researchers access linked survey-register data via a Statistics Denmark research project (no public download found; not located in the DDA catalogue).

The original DTUS 2001 CAPI questionnaire and diary form were not found online (searched VIVE/SFI, Rockwool Foundation, MTUS - which lists Denmark 2001 only as a harmonised simple file with no documentation - and Danish National Archives). Only partial verbatim question wording is available via Browning & Gørtz (2006); Bonke WP 26:2002 describes but does not quote the household-work items. The 1987 questionnaire documentation (MTUS readme) was not checked.

Used by: Taber and Vejlin (2020) · Waves used: 1995 and 2000

No questionnaire found online.

(2003, SJWEH 29(4):270-279) describe the 1990-2000 waves.

European Community Household Panel (ECHP); Danish part sometimes called Det Europæiske Husstandspanel

Eurostat, fielded in Denmark by the Danish National Institute of Social Research (SFI, now VIVE) · Survey home page

  • Documentation The EC Household Panel Newsletter (01/02), Eurostat 2003 (sample sizes by wave and country) English · 1994-1999 · 11 pages

Access: Anonymised ECHP UDB microdata via Eurostat's microdata access procedure for research entities; the register-linked Danish file is held by VIVE/Statistics Denmark and used on DST research servers.

No ECHP questionnaire (household/personal, generic or Danish) was found online. The Eurostat ECHP library (CIRCABC 'echpanel' Library, PAN documents) now requires EU Login; the EPUNet ECHP user guide site (epunet.essex.ac.uk) no longer resolves and the Wayback Machine is unreachable from this environment. International Social Survey Programme 2002: Family and Changing Gender Roles III (ISSP 2002, GESIS ZA3880; Danish component 'ISSP Family 2002')

ISSP Research Group; archived by GESIS (ZA3880). Danish component run by the Danish ISSP programme (Aalborg University, Jørgen Goul Andersen) · Survey home page

Cross-national attitude module on women's employment, family, childcare and division of housework fielded in 34 countries in 2002-03. In Denmark: simple random sample of 2,100 from the CPR register, postal self-completion survey with telephone follow-up, 21 Nov 2002 - 28 Feb 2003, 1,379 respondents (66.2% response). Survey of immigrants' education brought from abroad (Spørgeskemaundersøgelse om indvandrernes medbragte uddannelse), feeding the education register (HFAUDD/UDDA source 'survey')

Statistics Denmark (Danmarks Statistik) · Survey home page

Postal Danish-language questionnaire (with instructions in 12 languages) sent in September 1999 to about 159,000 immigrants aged 18-59 who arrived 1970-1998 without a Danish qualifying education, asking about schooling, vocational/higher education and work experience abroad; repeated for later arrival cohorts (to 2004, then from 2016/2017 via language schools, e-Boks web surveys and STAR CV data), with imputation for non-respondents.

Used by: Damm (2009), Dustmann et al. (2023), Jensen and Manning (2025) · Waves used: 1999 survey and later updates

  • Documentation Indvandrernes uddannelse (Henrik Mørkeberg, Danmarks Statistik 2000): results of the 1999 survey; Ch. 8 'Undersøgelsens tilrettelæggelse' describes the questionnaire content (8.4 Spørgeskema, 8.5 Vejledninger), data collection and coding Danish · 1999 · 99 pages · Chapter 8, from printed p. 71 (PDF p. 65); 8.4 Spørgeskema and 8.5 Vejledninger on printed pp. 73-74 (PDF pp. 67-68)
  • Documentation Indvandrernes medbragte uddannelse (Danmarks Statistik method note, 19 June 2018) Danish · 1999-2017 · 12 pages
  • Documentation Indvandrernes medbragte uddannelse (Danmarks Statistik method note, 11 February 2020): overview of all surveys 1999-2020, collection methods and imputation Danish · 1999-2020 · 16 pages

Access: Survey-based foreign education is embedded in Statistics Denmark's education register (UDDA/UDDF, variable HFAUDD with source codes), accessed through DST's Research Service.

The questionnaire form itself (1999 or later rounds) and the 12-language instruction sheets are not reproduced in any document found; the 2000 publication describes the questions (highest schooling, vocational/higher education, year completed, practical vs theoretical, work abroad) but does not print the form.

Custom online survey on social positions and fairness views (Hvidberg, Kreiner and Stantcheva)

Authors (Hvidberg, Kreiner, Stantcheva), CEBI, University of Copenhagen; invitations sent via Statistics Denmark · Survey home page

Online Qualtrics survey of Danes born 1969-1973 drawn from the population register and invited via Statistics Denmark (Feb-Mar 2019); elicits perceived income positions in several reference groups, fairness views, and includes a randomised information treatment on true positions. Incentivised online time-discounting experiment and survey (Epper, Fehr, Fehr-Duda, Kreiner, Lassen, Leth-Petersen and Rasmussen)

Authors (CEBI, University of Copenhagen / University of Zurich) · Survey home page

Online experiment run in 2015 with a random sample of middle-aged Danes (3,620 completers) invited by letter. It elicits time, risk and social preferences with real payouts to NemKonto and is linked to administrative wealth registers. A 2015 follow-up with 2,096 respondents and a 2018 study with 4,151 Danes born 1967-1986 also asked the 1973 DLSY preferred-income-profile question.

Used by: Epper et al. (2020) · Waves used: as fielded for the paper

  • Questions in appendix Time Discounting and Wealth Inequality - Online Appendix (AER 2020) English (with Danish invitation letter facsimile) · 2015 (and 2018 follow-up) · 31 pages · Online Appendix B.1, pp. 3-4 (invitation letter in Danish, with English translation); B.2, p. 5 (Table A1: all 15 intertemporal choice situations); D.1, pp. 22-24 (comparison with the DLSY survey question)
  • Questions quoted in paper Time Discounting and Wealth Inequality, CEBI Working Paper 08/19 (with appendices) English · 2015 · 67 pages · Section 2, pp. 10-11 (task description and Figure 1 screenshot of the allocation screen); Section 4.1, p. 26 (verbatim wording of the 1973 DLSY job/income-profile question, re-asked to the experiment subsample)

Access: Experimental data are linked to Danish administrative registers on Statistics Denmark's servers and are not public; access is through CEBI / Statistics Denmark research agreements (openICPSR project 115221 holds code only).

The appendix has no full screen-by-screen instructions, no video-instruction scripts and no risk- or social-preference task screens. The paper says each task had video instructions and comprehension questions, but these are not reproduced. The AEA zip (materials/12350) holds only disclosure statements.

Online survey of Danish UI benefit recipients, March 2018 (Altmann, Cairo, Mahlstedt and Sebald), used as auxiliary data on Joblog application coverage

Steffen Altmann, Sofie Cairo, Robert Mahlstedt, Alexander Sebald (University of Copenhagen), fielded via the Danish public employment service; data shared with Fluchtmann, Glenny, Harmon and Maibom · Survey home page

One-off online survey (lottery incentive) of a random sample of Danish UI recipients in March 2018, alongside an RCT on information about UI rules. It covered job-search behaviour, logging of applications in the Joblog system and knowledge of benefit rules, and was linked to Joblog and register data (about 1,236 respondents to the Joblog item).

Used by: Fluchtmann et al. (2024) · Waves used: as fielded for the paper

  • Questions in appendix Fluchtmann, Glenny, Harmon and Maibom, 'Unemployed Job Search across People and over Time', IZA DP 17048, Online Appendix B.6.1 English · March 2018 · 77 pages · Appendix B.6.1, pp. 50-51 (PDF pp. 52-53): Table 10 gives the verbatim question 'Which of these statements best describes your use of Joblog?' with its four answer options; Table 11 and the text describe the items on the number of applications sent last month and the number not registered in Joblog (paraphrased, not verbatim)
  • Questions in appendix Altmann, Cairo, Mahlstedt and Sebald, 'Do Job Seekers Understand the UI Benefit System (And Does It Matter)?', IZA DP 15747, Appendix A English (translation) · March 2018 pre-intervention survey and post-intervention survey · 66 pages · Appendix A.1, pp. 40-41 (survey implementation); A.2, pp. 41-42 (English translation of knowledge questions Q1-Q7 on UI benefit rules)

Access: Not publicly available. The survey was run by the Altmann et al. team and linked to Joblog/register data on Statistics Denmark's research servers; access is via the original authors.

No full questionnaire (Danish original or complete English version) is online. The Joblog-coverage items are reproduced only in Fluchtmann et al. Appendix B.6.1, and DP 15747 reproduces only the benefit-knowledge items. Fluchtmann et al. cite the source as Altmann, Cairo, Mahlstedt and Sebald (2019), 'Complexity and the Effectiveness of Public Policy' (also called Mahlstedt et al. 2019), which is the earlier title of IZA DP 15747.
